# Third Frat Night

## Clip Review

As the battle approaches, Peter carries the burden of leadership. He wears the title of High King, but in this moment the weight of it seems almost too much.

Then Edmund steps beside him. He doesn't compete with Peter. He doesn't remind him of his failures. He doesn't secretly enjoy seeing his older brother struggle. Instead, he simply encourages him.

Sometimes another man doesn't need advice as much as he needs a brother to remind him that he is capable of doing what God has already called him to do.

## Connection

If God is truly our Father, then the men around us are more than acquaintances or teammates. They are our brothers.

That sounds obvious until we remember how men often behave toward one another. We compare ourselves. We compete. Too often, we envy another man's gifts while hoping our own achievements receive more attention.

The Gospel offers something entirely different. God never forms great men in isolation. Moses needed Aaron and Hur to hold up his arms. Paul needed Barnabas. Even Our Lord surrounded Himself with twelve men whom He patiently formed into brothers.

I have found that some of the most important moments in my own life came because another man refused to let me carry a burden alone. Sometimes all he offered was a conversation. Sometimes it was a compliment, and sometimes it was a correction, but it was always encouraging.

Looking back, I can see that God was caring for me through my brothers. That is what Christian brotherhood is meant to be. Every one of us needs a brother like Edmund. And every one of us is called to become one.
