# Fourth Frat Night

## Clip Review

As the dragon approaches, fear spreads through the children. No one pretends the danger isn't real, but the father takes action against the threat.

"Not if I kill it first."

He doesn't make promises he can't keep. He doesn't deny the existence of evil. He simply places himself between the danger and those entrusted to his care.

Here we see the heart of fatherhood.

## Connection

Over these past weeks we've been walking through the stages of becoming a man. We began with maturity, learning to accept responsibility instead of avoiding it. Then we discovered that mature men first learn to live as sons, receiving their identity and mission from the Father. From there we learned that sons become brothers, carrying one another's burdens instead of trying to carry everything alone.

All of those lessons lead here.

Fatherhood is the mature form of manhood itself.

A father is a man who has stopped asking, "Who will take care of me?" and begun asking, "Whom has God entrusted to my care?" Some men will live that vocation through marriage and children. Others through the priesthood. Others by mentoring younger men or faithfully serving their communities. But every Christian man is called to become the sort of man who willingly walks toward the dragon while others find safety behind him.

That is precisely what God has done for us. The Good Shepherd did not remove every danger from the world. He stepped between His people and death itself. That is the pattern of every Christian father.

## Commission

We need to ask ourselves two questions.

First, who has God entrusted to my care? Is my heart on that, or is it focused on something or someone else? If you're a young man, this might seem strange, but I would challenge you to look around. These are your brothers. Do you care for them or only care for yourself?

Second, what dragon have I been hoping someone else would fight? Am I becoming the man that is trained and ready to step in, or am I still just taking care of myself?

Face the dragon. That is what fathers do.

## Squad Time

### Opening Prayer

Open with a simple thanksgiving, asking God to bless the time. An Our Father and a Hail Mary is appropriate.

### Reading

> "He will turn the heart of fathers to their sons, and the heart of sons to their fathers, lest I come and strike the land with utter destruction." (Malachi 4:6)

> "Fathers, do not provoke your children to anger, but bring them up with the training and instruction of the Lord." (Ephesians 6:4)

- Why do you think fathers need to be 'turned' toward their children? What turns us away?
- What is the difference between 'training' a son and 'provoking' him to anger?

### Wisdom from the Ages

> "The more intense the love, the less we think of the sacrifice involved to secure what we love." (Fulton J. Sheen)

- Why do we need to learn to love in order to learn to sacrifice?
- Think of the self-denial of a pro athlete. What makes them so dedicated — and how would that apply to us?
