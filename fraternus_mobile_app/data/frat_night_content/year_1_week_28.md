# Generosity vs Greed

## Clip Review

In this scene from The Treasure of the Sierra Madre, three men have struck gold in the mountains, and each is hiding his own share. Dobbs has buried his under a rock. His partner Curtin has been poking around that same rock, not for the gold, he says, but because he saw a gila monster, a venomous lizard, crawl underneath it. Dobbs doesn't believe him. He's sure Curtin is lying, sure he's reaching for the gold, and he pulls a gun on him and calls him a thief. Howard, the old prospector, tries to talk them both down. Finally they agree there's only one way to settle it, and that is to turn the rock over and see what's really under there.

## Connection

Curtin was telling the truth the whole time. He wasn't after Dobbs's gold at all; he was trying to keep his partner from getting a poisonous bite. But greed had overcome Dobbs, and he couldn't tell the difference between a man trying to rob him and a man trying to protect him. But notice that the treasure was in the same hole as the lizard. What he was protecting and what could poison him were under the same rock.

Greed doesn't just make a man want more; it makes him alone even when he is among his fellow men.

Generosity is open-handedness, with your money, yes, but also with your time, your attention, your strength, the credit you could keep for yourself. It starts with seeing that whatever you have was handed to you first, and that it's meant to move through you instead of piling up behind you.

Greed is the opposite because it's the hand that closes. And a man whose hand is always closed doesn't grow or mature. He shrinks. He closes in on himself. He starts measuring every gift by what he'll get back, guarding his little pile until, like Dobbs, he can't even recognize his own brothers anymore.

As you work through The Daily Sword&Spade this week, watch for the small moments when your hand wants to close: when you could share, and you'd rather keep, when you could give the better part away and something in you says mine first. It's rarely dramatic. It's often a decision no one else would even notice, and that's the ground where this battle is won or lost.

## Commission

We don't have to live with a closed fist because we have been given so much. Everything you have, your life, your family, this brotherhood, the very breath in your chest, is a gift. God is the first giver, and He didn't hold back the best part. A real man can be generous because he isn't scraping by; he has confidence that his Father provides. So this week, loosen your grip. Give the better part away. Share the credit. Be generous with your time. You won't come out with less in the end. Instead, you'll come out more like your Father, who gives and gives and is never once diminished. Greed asks only what's mine, but generosity already knows all of life is a gift.
