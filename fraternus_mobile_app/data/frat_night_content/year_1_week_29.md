# Kindness vs Envy

## Clip Review

In this scene from The Treasure of the Sierra Madre, two partners who set out together to dig for gold are alone in the dark, and Dobbs has a gun on Curtin. He tells Curtin things have changed, that he's in charge now, that he's only doing what he has to do to save his own skin. Then he lays out the plan: he's going to kill Curtin, keep all the gold for himself, and pin the theft on the dead man, so the old prospector comes hunting for Curtin instead of him. Then he forces Curtin up at gunpoint and marches him off into the dark toward what he calls his funeral.

## Connection

This moment is so disturbing because the man at the wrong end of that gun is supposed to be the decent one. Earlier in the story, when the mine caved in and buried Dobbs alive, it was Curtin who crawled into the collapsing tunnel and dragged him out. Curtin saved his life, and this is the thanks he gets.

But look closely at his plan. He doesn't just want to kill Curtin and take the gold. He wants to make Curtin the thief. He wants the good man to go down as the bad one. That is the black heart of envy. Greed wants the gold, but envy can't stand the good in another man, so it sets to work tearing that good down.

A corrupt man can't bear to stand next to a decent one, because the decent man is a mirror, and the mirror shames him. So instead of admiring the good, instead of letting it change him, envy tries to blacken it. It talks the good man down. Envy hunts for his flaws. It would rather the honest man be exposed as secretly rotten than admit that he's better.

Notably, envy almost never shows its face directly. It hides behind a joke at a friend's expense, a little dig, a cooling off when he wins, a rumor passed along with a shrug. It is the one sin that brings no pleasure at all.

Kindness is the cure because it takes far more strength. Kindness isn't merely being polite; it's willing another man's good and then doing something to bring it about. Where envy sinks at your brother's success, kindness rises to meet it. It defends a man's name when he isn't in the room.

As you work through The Daily Sword&Spade this week, watch for the small souring when a brother is praised, and you are not. Don't pretend it isn't there. Name the vice in your prayers and confession. The counterattack is almost always something small and concrete: say the kind word out loud and to his face, defend him when someone starts tearing him down, do the very thing envy is telling you to withhold.

## Commission

Here's why we can afford to be glad when another man is good, or blessed, or ahead of us: there is no shortage of kindness from our Father. Envy runs on a lie, the lie that there's only so much good to go around, so every bit your brother gets was somehow stolen from you. It isn't true. God is not a Father with limits on his kindness. He gives to each of you fully and differently, and your brother's gift takes nothing from yours.

Remember the older brother in the story of the prodigal son, so sour at his brother's welcome-home feast that he wouldn't go inside. The Father came out to meet him where he was, too.

Be kind on purpose this week. When a brother wins, go in and celebrate. Speak well of him when his back is turned. Guard his good name as if it were your own. You lose nothing by it, and you become more like your Father, who delights to give and has never once run short.
