# Temperance vs Gluttony


## Clip Review

In this scene from The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King, the city of Minas Tirith is bracing for war. Soldiers ride out to defend it, many of them straight toward their deaths, while the enemy's armies mass in the dark. And where is Denethor, the Steward of Gondor, the man who is supposed to be leading them? He is sitting in a hall, eating. While his men ride out to die, he tears into his meal, juice running down his chin, barely seeming to notice, as young Pippin stands beside him singing a low and mournful song. The film cuts back and forth between the three: the soldiers riding to slaughter, the orcs drawing their bows, and the steward feeding his face.

## Connection

Denethor is the Steward of Gondor, or at least he is supposed to be. A steward is not the king, but he is the man trusted to govern the kingdom on the king's behalf, to hold it in good order until the king returns. But how do we find him? The city is under attack, his own men are riding to their deaths on his command, his own son is among them, and he cannot lift his eyes from his plate.

Gluttony is an appetite that has slipped its reins and taken over the man. It's a type of hunger that serves nothing but itself. And notice how it numbs Denethor to everything around him. He eats himself into indifference while everything he is responsible for burns. That's one danger of this vice. It softens a man, but it also blinds him.

Temperance is the virtue we need. Too often, it is confused with a grim hatred of good food and good things. But in reality, a temperate man can feast better than anyone, precisely because the feast doesn't own him. He enjoys his appetites, but they don't rule him. Temperance is what keeps a man awake and free.

Gluttony is any appetite that won't take no for an answer: the screen you can't set down, the feed that never ends, the voice that always says just one more, feed me now, no matter the cost or who is waiting on you.

As you pray through The Daily Sword&Spade this week, watch for the moment your appetite says more, and you know the right answer is that you have had enough.

## Commission

Here's the good news: your appetites are not your enemy. God made them, and He made them good. Hunger, delight, a good meal shared with good men, these are gifts. They were simply never meant to be your master.

Look at Christ. He fasted forty days in the desert and would not turn a stone to bread on the devil's request, and He also sat and ate with sinners so freely that His enemies sneered and falsely called Him a glutton. That is a free man. He was master of His appetite in both directions, able to go without and able to enjoy, because neither one owned Him. That is the freedom He wants you to live in.
