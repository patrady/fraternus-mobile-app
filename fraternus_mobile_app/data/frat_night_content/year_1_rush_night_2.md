# Second Frat Night

## Clip Review

King Arthur and his knights stand before their enemies and a frozen lake that could give way beneath them at any moment. Every step forward is uncertain.

What is remarkable isn't simply their courage, but their trust. No one argues or breaks ranks. They know whose command they are under, and because they trust the one leading them, they are willing to walk where they would never have gone alone.

## Connection

Last week we talked about accepting responsibility and maturity. That naturally raises another question.

Who is giving us our responsibilities in the first place?

The oldest temptation in the world is the temptation to become our own master. We want to decide for ourselves what is good, what is worth sacrificing for, and where our lives ought to go.

Christianity begins somewhere very different. Before we are husbands, fathers, leaders, or mentors, we are sons.

Consider Jesus. Again and again He points to His Father. He receives His mission from the Father, entrusts Himself to the Father's will, and even teaches us to begin our own prayer with two simple words: "Our Father."

When I forget that God is my Father, I begin living like an orphan. If I do serve God, I do it like a slave not a son. Every burden feels as though it rests entirely on my shoulders and every failure threatens my identity. If we dig, most of us really don't know who we are, because we haven't let the One who can actually tell us speak to us.

But sons don't live that way. A son knows he is loved before he has accomplished anything. He receives before he achieves. He trusts before he understands. And that trust is what gives him courage.

## Commission

This week, ask yourself a simple question.

Am I living more like an orphan or more like a son?

Brothers, if you have ever felt like God is just a distant judge or law-giver, or if you think of "being good" as just trying to not get in trouble with God, you are missing the whole point.

Over and over God is asking you to accept who you are and stop - right now - trying to invent your own identity. Last week we said we're aiming for maturity. To really start there we have to accept who we are. Because to grow in virtue is to become more of who you are. Will you accept your Father's offer to come more deeply into His love for you?

## Squad Time

### Opening Prayer

Open with a simple thanksgiving, asking God to bless the time. An Our Father and a Hail Mary are appropriate.

### Reading

> "For those who are led by the Spirit of God are children of God. For you did not receive a spirit of slavery to fall back into fear, but you received a spirit of adoption, through which we cry, "Abba, Father!" The Spirit itself bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God, and if children, then heirs, heirs of God and joint heirs with Christ, if only we suffer with him so that we may also be glorified with him" (Rom. 8:14–17)

- Before thinking about this in spiritual terms: what are some of the fundamental differences between being a 'slave' to another man and an 'heir' to a father's estate? *(["What else?" is a great question to keep it going once answers start.])*
- What does it look like when we approach God with a 'servile' or 'slave-like' attitude?

### Wisdom from the Ages

> "He who believes in Christ becomes a son of God. This filial adoption transforms him by giving him the ability to follow the example of Christ. It makes him capable of acting rightly and doing good. In union with his Savior, the disciple attains the perfection of charity, which is holiness. Having matured in grace, the moral life blossoms into eternal life in the glory of heaven." (Catechism 1709)

- Have you ever met someone — or experienced within yourself — a real 'transformation' in life because of God?
- What do you think keeps most men from living like a child of God? Or, what are we scared will happen if we live totally for God?
