# First Frat Night

## Clip Review

In this scene, Edmund, Lucy, and Eustace are unexpectedly called back to Narnia. None of them planned the journey. None of them asked for it. They simply find themselves invited into another adventure.

What becomes obvious almost immediately is that they don't all receive the invitation the same way. Edmund and Lucy step into this strange new world with wonder. Eustace spends every second complaining. He criticizes the ship, questions the crew, mocks their customs, and starts making demands.

If you know the story, the difference in reaction is because Edmund and Lucy have been matured through past experience. Eustace doesn't see he lacks the virtue that comes with maturity.

## Connection

There is something of Eustace in every one of us.

When we're young, we imagine maturity means finally getting to live life on our own terms. We've been shuttled around and protected our whole life, but when we're "adults" we can do what we want. We look forward to the day when no one can tell us what to do, and we think freedom means being able to do what we want.

Then real life begins.

A difficult task lands in your lap, your family needs more from you than you expected, a child is born, a friend asks for help at the worst possible time, your parish needs volunteers, your parents begin growing older… The list goes on.

Little by little you discover that becoming a man is not escaping responsibility, but becoming the type that knows how to carry it. Virtue is not a restriction on you as a man. It is the truth of who you are grown up and trained into authentic manhood. To do that is to ask for responsibility, to be capable of carrying it, not running away from it.

That's a key difference between a boy and a man. A boy asks, "Why should I have to?" A man begins asking, "What is God asking of me here?"

Looking back over my own life, I can see that the moments when I grew the most were rarely the moments I chose for myself. Instead, they were the moments that interrupted my plans. At the time, they felt inconvenient, but years later, I realized they were invitations. For Eustace, the adventure was an invitation to real life.

God has a remarkable way of disguising growth as responsibility.

## Commission

This week we are placing this invitation before you. This brotherhood is not about having fun and enjoying company. It is about growing in virtue. For us as Catholics, that isn't self-help. Virtue is not an end, it is a means. Our end is God. We are going to go toward Him, together.

The invitation is simple, will you be joining us this year? In four weeks we'll commit at the Challenge Ceremony on [date]. For now, let's go to Squad Time to lay some ground rules.

## Squad Time

### Opening Prayer

Open with a simple thanksgiving, asking God to bless the time. An Our Father and a Hail Mary are appropriate.

### Welcome

Welcome the boys to Fraternus and to their first Squad Time. Introduce yourself briefly, without much detail. Ask if they have any thoughts about the movie clip or the King's Message.

### Ground Rules

Explain that Squad Time can be one of the best parts of Fraternus — provided we follow a few simple and manly rules. Say the rule, let it land, then say, "Before we agree to this rule, let me ask you a couple of questions."

#### Rule 1: Squad Time is always real.

*[Pause and let it sink in.]*

- Without using names, describe a faker.
- Why is fakeness annoying to men?
- Can we all agree not to be fake? (Yes or no is fine here.)

#### Rule 2: Respect your Squad.

*(Keep it cool and positive.) [Pause and let it sink in.]*

- Is there a place in your life where you don't feel respected?
- Can we all agree to be respectful of everyone here?

#### Rule 3: What's said in Squad Time stays in Squad Time.

*[Pause and let it sink in.]*

> The only exception: some situations, if presented, must be reported to the proper authorities. If that worries someone, he may be in a situation that desperately needs intervention. This rule pertains to the sins of gossip and slander, not to appropriate communication with parents generally.

- How do you feel when you share something in confidence and it turns into gossip?
- Can we all agree not to be gossips, and to keep Squad Time confidential?

### Introduction Questions

Once the ground rules are set, spend the rest of the time getting to know each other.

1. Ask each young man his name, where he goes to school, and something light — a best summer memory, an interesting fact about himself.
2. Ask why he came to Fraternus and what he's expecting from it. (You do not need to comment on this part.)

### Challenge

This week's challenge is to explain and show the new daily Sword&Spade guide to spiritual combat. Tell them how to join as a member at the Fraternus website, and to come to you with any concerns or financial needs. Make sure they know that once they join, their guide arrives in the mail, refills come automatically, and Sword&Spade magazine comes with it — and encourage them to join before the Challenge Ceremony on [date].

Logistics live here, in the Challenge — not in the King's Message. Give directions on Squads if that isn't well established in the Chapter yet. Have Captains ready to call their grades/groups and move to Squad Time as quickly as possible after the King's Message.
